WHERE DID ALL THE SEDANS GO?

Sources, Methods & Credits

A data storytelling project by Pierre Zhaopeng Zhang.

Three measures. Three distinct views.

  1. Registered fleet: active light-duty registrations, including older vehicles. Counts are shown in millions. Statistics Canada ↗ (opens in a new tab); Table 23-10-0308-01.
  2. New registrations: multi-purpose vehicles as a share of all new motor vehicle registrations in each stated year. Statistics Canada: Vehicle registrations, 2025 ↗ (opens in a new tab), September 22, 2026.
  3. Platform searches: share of Canadian searches on AutoTrader, January 1–October 21, 2024. AutoTrader 2024 report ↗ (opens in a new tab). Category definitions and populations are not interchangeable with Statistics Canada’s.

Methods, source versions & asset credits

Fleet lines join nine actual annual observations; displayed counts are rounded to one decimal million. New-registration shares use only two published endpoints, with a difference of 63.3 − 42.1 = 21.2 percentage points. The September 2026 release reports 63.3%; an earlier March release reported 63.2%. The cause of that difference was not independently reconciled. The later release is used consistently here.

AutoTrader’s remaining search share is approximately 11% (100 − 49 − 40), calculated from rounded inputs. No conversion rate, national preference estimate, causal attribution or nationwide nameplate total is calculated.

Vehicle images are AI-assisted editorial illustrations based on individually licensed references, with model-specific credits and licence terms on the Sources, Methods & Credits page. Definitions and limitations

Passenger cars are a broader statistical category than the sedan in Pierre’s scenario. Multi-purpose vehicles include SUVs and crossovers. The two highlighted categories are not the whole fleet. The source table excludes unknown vehicle types or gross vehicle weight ratings, trailers and off-road categories; the chart also excludes Other vehicles. Methods changed in 2017, so this story starts there.

Counts are published in thousands, to one decimal (approximately 100 vehicles), then divided by 1,000 for the million-vehicle display. Lines connect annual observations; they do not measure within-year changes. The first observed crossover is 2022, subject to Ontario’s administrative changes. These data do not establish a sudden change in consumer psychology.

Statistics Canada Table 23-10-0308-01 · Vehicle type definitions · Earlier March 2026 release · Statistics Canada Open Licence

AutoTrader measures searches, not unique people or purchases, across a new/used marketplace. Its Cars label is not interchangeable with Statistics Canada’s passenger-car category. AutoTrader’s accompanying report provides context.

The eight vehicle cases are independently selected examples, not matched replacements or a nationwide count. Model years, production dates, retail withdrawals and first deliveries are distinct. Fleet-only qualifications, uncertain delivery dates and individual factual source links remain beside every model in the story.
